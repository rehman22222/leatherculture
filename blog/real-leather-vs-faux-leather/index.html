Real leather or not? Four checks before you pay

Plenty of jackets in the market are sold as genuine leather when they are coated fabric. You can tell in about a minute.

Walk through any market here and half the jackets on the rack will be called genuine leather. A good number of them are not. You do not need to be an expert to check, and you do not need to take the shopkeeper's word for it either. Four things, one minute.

1. Look closely at the grain

Real leather is irregular. The pores, creases and little marks never repeat, because it came off an animal. Faux leather is printed, so if you look at two spots a few inches apart you will see the same pattern twice. This is the quickest tell.

2. Press it with your thumb

Push down firmly. Leather wrinkles and gives slightly around your thumb, then settles back. Synthetic stays flat and bounces straight back with no creasing at all.

3. Check a cut edge

Look at a hem, a cuff or inside a pocket opening. A real leather edge looks fibrous, slightly rough, almost furry. Faux leather is fabric with a coating on top, so the edge shows a clean plastic layer sitting over cloth.

4. Smell it

The easiest one. Leather smells rich and a bit sweet. Synthetic smells of chemicals or plastic no matter how it has been treated. If you are not sure, compare it with a leather belt or a pair of leather shoes you already own.

About the price

Leather has a floor price because the hide itself costs money before anyone has cut or stitched it. A full leather jacket going for two or three thousand rupees is almost certainly coated fabric. That is not automatically a bad buy, but you should know what you are paying for, and you should not pay leather prices for it.
